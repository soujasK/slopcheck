Old vs new tax regime: which one should salaried people pick?

By Priya Sharma

The choice between the two regimes depends mostly on how many deductions a taxpayer can actually claim. Under the old regime, deductions for provident fund contributions, insurance premiums, home loan interest and house rent allowance can reduce taxable income substantially, while the new regime offers lower slab rates but removes most of those deductions.

Tax advisers interviewed for this article said that the break-even point varies with salary structure. A salaried employee who claims the full deduction under section 80C, pays rent in a metro city and services a home loan will often still pay less under the old regime. Someone with few investments and no rent or loan is usually better off under the new regime.

The official tax calculator on the income tax portal lets taxpayers compare both regimes with their own numbers, and advisers recommend running it before declaring a choice to the employer at the start of the financial year. Income tax calculator

Employees can usually change the declaration when filing the return, but those with business income face restrictions on switching back and forth between the regimes, so the decision deserves some care rather than a default selection.
