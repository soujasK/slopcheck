What the low-quality results have in common

Signals that fired on results in the low band (a result can show several):

  • no named author: 94 results
  • no author or date: 87 results
  • title and headings cover 0% of the query's words: 59 results
  • main text covers 0% of the query's words: 57 results
  • the site sells products on this page: 24 results
  • title and headings cover 33% of the query's words: 11 results

In this sample the classic affiliate-spam signals (affiliate links, "best X" listicles) did not predict low quality; anonymity, off-query pages and sellers' own content did.

Method and limitations

  • One snapshot (2026-10-03), one location, via SerpApi; not a trend.
  • Bing could not be measured (SerpApi's Bing returned unusable India results).
  • 510 of 660 non-platform results were scored; the rest are reported as unscored (robots.txt, 403/402, timeouts), never guessed.
  • Signals are heuristics; the rubric's weights were fitted on 155 LLM-labeled development items and tested once on a fresh held-out set.
  • English queries only. Labels from two LLMs with no human validation.
  • Aggregates only: no per-site verdicts.
