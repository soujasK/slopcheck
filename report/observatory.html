What the low-quality results have in common

Signals that fired on results in the low band (a result can show several):

  • no named author: 36 results
  • no author or date: 29 results
  • the site sells products on this page: 24 results
  • no about or contact page linked: 11 results
  • title and headings cover 33% of the query's words: 9 results
  • main text covers 33% of the query's words: 9 results

In this sample the classic affiliate-spam signals (affiliate links, "best X" listicles) did not predict low quality; anonymity, off-query pages and sellers' own content did.

Validation

Labels come from two LLMs (Gemini, gpt-oss-120b), not humans, judging blind; ground truth is where both agree. Full protocol and history: EVAL.md, LABELING.md, ANALYSIS_PLAN.md.

Round 2 test (rubric v2): gate not met

Precision 0.37 [0.19, 0.59], recall 0.35, F1 0.36, n = 71. It did not beat the one-line baseline "no author and no date". This test split was then retired and its labels became development data.

Round 3 fresh held-out test (rubric v3): gate not met

Precision 0.57 [0.25, 0.84], recall 0.19 [0.08, 0.40], F1 0.29, specificity 0.97, n = 114. Baseline "no author and no date": precision 0.15, F1 0.12.

Round 4 fresh held-out test (rubric v4, current): gate met

Precision 0.88 [0.53, 0.98], recall 0.30 [0.16, 0.51], F1 0.45, specificity 0.99, n = 102. Baseline "no author and no date": precision 0.43, F1 0.47 (higher F1 than the rubric).

Method and limitations

  • One snapshot (2026-10-03), one location, via SerpApi; not a trend.
  • Bing could not be measured (SerpApi's Bing returned unusable India results).
  • 490 of 660 non-platform results were scored; the rest are reported as unscored (robots.txt, 403/402, timeouts), never guessed.
  • Signals are heuristics; the rubric's weights were fitted on 155 LLM-labeled development items and tested once on a fresh held-out set.
  • English queries only. Labels from two LLMs with no human validation.
  • Aggregates only: no per-site verdicts.
